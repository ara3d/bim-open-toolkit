BIM Open Notebook brand proposals

Three marks, four accent candidates, and the family lockup for the notebook, the product the repository-layout proposal (section 5.1) gives its own repository. Everything else is inherited from docs/BRANDING.md: Instrument Sans, Public Sans and Fira Code; the neutrals; the status colours; a 24 × 24 single-path mark. Each mark is shown beside the flow mark, Rows to Flow, because the two will sit together in tab strips, start pages and papers.

Recommendation

Take mark N1, Flow to Page, in ink violet (#5a47c7), with the three-word family lockup BIM Open Notebook. Flow to Page is the flow mark read onward: the solid node that ends Rows to Flow is where this mark begins, and its wire lands in a page of alternating request lines and embed blocks. The two marks share the same disc, the same bar weight and the same left-to-right reading, so side by side they explain the relationship the proposal states: the notebook is a client of the flow host, and an answer becomes part of a document. Ink violet keeps the pair apart in a favicon row, which is the one place the guide records a weakness for the flow mark, and stays clear of the status triple and the canvas stripe hues.

  • Mark N2, Ribbon Page, is the most literal "notebook" and the best at 48 px and above, but its cut-outs close up at 16 px, the same failing the flow proposals found in Block Flow.
  • Mark N3, the N monogram, is the most distinct in a tab strip and the least informative; it says "N" before it says "notebook", and it borrows nothing from the flow mark except the disc.
  • Below 16 px, N1 uses the page alone (the spine and its rows) so that the notebook and flow favicons differ even when the node is lost.
  • The three-word lockup raises a decision for the flow brand: "BimOpenFlow" should become "BIM Open Flow" in the same step, or the notebook should be written "BimOpenNotebook". Section 4 shows both.

Mark N1: Flow to Page

mark-n1-flow-to-page.svg, lockup-n1-flow-to-page.svg (single fill, nonzero)

The flow mark continued. The disc on the left is the flow mark's node, same radius; one wire leaves it and joins the spine of a page on the right. Down the page hang two short lines and two wide blocks in turn: a request in plain language, then the embed the agent answered with, then the next request. The rhythm is the notebook's structure, turn after turn. The spine does the work at small sizes: at 16 px the lines thin to a hairline but the spine and the two blocks hold a page-like silhouette, and the disc keeps the family likeness. Below 16 px drop the node and the wire and use the page alone.

Mark N2: Ribbon Page

mark-n2-ribbon-page.svg, lockup-n2-ribbon-page.svg (single fill, evenodd)

A solid page with a bookmark ribbon notched out of its top right corner, and cut into the page a request line above a node with its wire: the answer, pinned inside the record. It is the only one of the three that a stranger would name "notebook" without a caption, and the solid field makes it the strongest at 48 and 96 px, on a start page or the first slide of a talk. The weakness is the one the flow proposals already met: the node, the wire and the line are negative space, and at 16 px they merge into a grey smudge inside a rectangle. It also shares no geometry with the flow mark beyond the disc, so the pair reads as two products rather than one family.

Mark N3: N monogram

mark-n3-n-monogram.svg, lockup-n3-n-monogram.svg (single fill, nonzero)

A capital N built from the flow mark's parts: two stems the weight of the table rows, a diagonal the weight of a wire, and the right stem ending in the node disc, so the letter is itself a small graph that terminates in an answer. The flow proposals ranked the monogram direction best for favicon legibility, and the same holds here: at 16 px it is the most distinct of the three, and it cannot be mistaken for the flow mark. What it lacks is meaning; it says the product's initial, not what the product is, and a reader who has not seen the flow mark gets nothing from the disc. It would suit a family in which every product has a monogram, which the flow decision did not choose.

2. Accent

The flow accent is #2f66ce. The notebook needs a hue that is clearly not that one in a 16 px favicon, is not green, amber or red (the status colours, which the notebook uses for "result matches the snapshot" and "result changed"), and stays away from the canvas stripes (teal, purple, orange) as far as a single hue can. Each tile shows the N1 mark, the flow mark beside it, a primary button, and a selected table row in the soft tint.

Ink violet #5a47c7 (recommended)
Reads as written record; far enough from the flow blue to tell apart at 16 px, close enough to feel like the same family. Soft tint #ece8fb.

3. A notebook page in the family type

Same three faces as the studio. What changes is the scale: a notebook is read, not operated, so the request is 15 px medium, the reply 14.5 px regular on a 1.55 line height and a 68-character measure, the folded tool calls 11.5 px Fira Code, and the title 20 px Instrument Sans. Embeds keep the studio's 12 to 13 px table type, so a table looks the same in both products. The amber dot is the existing status colour, used for an embed whose re-evaluated result differs from its snapshot.

4. Wordmark forms

Three ways to write the name beside the mark, each at 15 px as the command bar sets it. The guide writes the flow product as one word; the repositories are bim-open-flow and bim-open-notebook. Whichever form the notebook takes, the flow lockup should take the same one, or the two bars will disagree.

BIM Open Notebook
Family first, product second, two weights. Recommended. The flow bar would read BIM Open Flow.
BimOpenNotebook
Matches today's "BimOpenFlow" exactly. Fifteen letters in one word; at 15 px it is a long run with no break, and "Notebook" is harder to find in it than "Flow" is.
BimOpenNotebook
One word with the middle dimmed, the variant the flow proposals showed as "a dim Bim prefix". Keeps the single word and gives the eye a seam; still long.
