BimOpenFlow brand proposals

Three marks and two font pairings for the studio's command bar (22 px mark, 15 px wordmark), favicon (16 px) and start page (48 px). Palette as in the studio: accent #2f66ce, text #171a1f, dim #5a606c. The current isometric cube is shown for reference.

Recommendation

Take mark 3, Wired Block, with pairing A, Space Grotesk + Inter + JetBrains Mono. The Wired Block keeps the isometric cube people already see in the command bar, but draws it the way the product works: vertices are nodes, edges are wires, and the open frame says the tables are not locked away; it is the only one of the three whose 16 px favicon still reads as a distinct shape rather than a blob with holes. Pairing A changes one font only: Inter stays for every button and table cell, so nothing in the studio moves, while Space Grotesk gives the wordmark a squarer, more engineered outline that the geometric mark sits well beside. If the wordmark should stay quieter, pairing B (Instrument Sans with IBM Plex) is the calmer fallback at the cost of swapping the UI face.

  • Mark 1, Block Flow, is the strongest at 48 px and above and the weakest at 16 px, where its three cut-out nodes close up.
  • Mark 2, Rows to Flow, explains the product best on a start page but looks like a generic merge icon in a tab strip.

1. Logo concepts

Reference: the current mark

A plain isometric cube: a building block, nothing about tables or flow. Any of the three proposals below drops into the same slot as a single path in accent blue.

Mark 1: Block Flow

mark-1-block-flow.svg, lockup-1-block-flow.svg (single fill, fill-rule evenodd)

A stepped block silhouette, three volumes of different heights standing for a building, with a wire cut straight through it: three nodes joined in a row. The building is the data, the wire is the question passing through it. The nodes are negative space, so the mark is one path in one colour and works reversed on the accent. Below 22 px the cut-outs narrow to a dotted line, which is honest about how it will look as a favicon.

Mark 2: Rows to Flow

mark-2-rows-to-flow.svg, lockup-2-rows-to-flow.svg (single fill, nonzero)

The abstract concept: three table rows on the left, their wires bending into one node on the right. It draws the product's claim, tables are the currency and a graph turns them into an answer, without a building at all, which suits the toolkit's non-architectural users (developers, the MCP agent). The curves are the only non-orthogonal geometry in the set and give the mark movement; the node is a full disc so it stays solid at 16 px. Its weakness is familiarity: as a tab icon it can pass for a generic "merge" or "git" glyph.

Mark 3: Wired Block

mark-3-wired-block.svg, lockup-3-wired-block.svg (single fill, nonzero; all subpaths wound clockwise so overlaps stay solid)

The existing isometric cube redrawn as a graph: its seven visible vertices become nodes and its nine edges become wires, so the block is hollow and open, an outline you can see through to the data inside. It keeps continuity with what people already see in the command bar and in the 3D pane, and it is the mark that survives 16 px best, because the outer hexagon holds the shape even when the nodes shrink to bumps. At 48 px and up the nodes read clearly and the mark could take a second colour on the centre node (a pack hue, or green for "every node green") without a second path.

2. Font pairings

Each block shows the wordmark at 15 px bold beside the mark, a page heading at 18 px, body text at 13 px, a step row (title 13 px, description 12 px, parameter line 11 px monospace) and button labels at 13 px 600. Fonts come from fonts.googleapis.com; Consolas is the system fallback that the current studio uses.

Baseline (current): Inter for everything, Consolas for monospace

What the studio ships today. Inter is a fine UI face and disappears at 13 px, which is the point; the wordmark in Inter Bold is the weakness, since it is the same face as every button label and has no identity of its own. Consolas is Windows-only, so the monospace lines look different on a Mac or in CI screenshots.

Pairing A: Space Grotesk (wordmark and page headings) + Inter (UI) + JetBrains Mono

Space Grotesk has squared bowls and a flat-sided "O" and "p" that match the isometric geometry of the marks; at 15 px bold "BimOpenFlow" reads as a name rather than a label. It is used only for the wordmark and the page heading, so Inter keeps every table cell, button and step title exactly as they are today, and the change costs one font file. JetBrains Mono replaces Consolas: it is the same on every platform, its zero is slashed, and at 11 px its letterforms stay open, which matters on the parameter line where limit=null and n_7f3a must be read exactly.

Pairing B: Instrument Sans (wordmark) + IBM Plex Sans (UI) + IBM Plex Mono

The calmer option. Instrument Sans is a grotesque with slightly narrow, even letters; at 15 px bold it is quieter than Space Grotesk and closer to an engineering title block than to a start-up header. IBM Plex Sans replaces Inter throughout: it has a little more contrast and a more open "a" and "g", which makes 12 px descriptions on the dim colour easier to read, and it ships with a sibling monospace, so parameter lines and body text share x-height and weight and a step row looks like one typeface in two cuts. The cost is that every UI string changes width by a few percent, so the studio's layouts would need one pass for wrapping.
