# STEP mesher status, 2026-10-21

Written at the owner's request after the type catalog merge (ae93d132). It answers: what is done, what remains, what was learned, where the work stalled, and how far the epic is from its goals. The plan it reports on is `docs/plans/step-mesher-v2.md` (chunks, ledger, rule changes); the epic is TKT-323; the last measurement is `docs/plans/step-mesher-corpus-2026-10-19.md`.

## Goals

TKT-323: a STEP B-rep mesher whose geometry is written in Plato, compiled to C#, called from the C# STEP mesher, and shown in the toolkit's 3D pane. The owner fixed the shape on 2026-10-17 (TKT-306): C# parses (`Ara3D.IO.StepParser`), types (`Ara3D.StepTypes`, generated from EXPRESS) and decodes the file as written (`RawDecodedModel`); Plato does all geometry, meshing, validation, editing and repair (`sandbox/step-brep-mesh`, layers 00 to 11); laws are the tests, and the Plato interpreter and the C# backend must agree law by law.

The epic's acceptance criteria:

1. chunks A to H written in Plato, their laws passing, no throwaway C# body left;
2. every chunk's laws run on both engines and agree;
3. `Ara3D.StepBrep` regenerated by one documented script and called by `StepMesher.Mesh` for every B-rep;
4. a public AP214 or AP242 sample meshes closed with bounds matching its units, and the 88 u-blox files mesh with zero exceptions, with a per-surface-kind table and run time;
5. one meshed part captured in the 3D pane.

It serves workflows 3 (a picture of the model) and 4 (a STEP node pack) of `PROJECT.md`, which does not name STEP yet; TKT-306 still asks which workflow STEP belongs to.

## Hurdles

- **Writer trust.** A law result on the C# backend counted only once each writer defect had a regression test. Six writer fixes, the List-first defect, the `MapRange` recomputation, and a generic-inference defect that appears only when the program is large enough (41 minutes to bisect) each cost a session.
- **Interpreter speed.** Hundreds of times slower than compiled code: 72 point insertions took 41 s after the refinement fix. 50 laws at the 120 s budget cost 100 of a 107-minute sweep, and every wave 1 merge paid a 12 to 120 minute sweep. The owner's answer on 2026-10-19: a 2 s budget, laws on compiled C#, expensive values computed once and shared.
- **Coordination in version 1.** One compiled program: one agent's missing semicolon reported "0 laws, 0 type errors" for everyone (TKT-337). Shared files: eight cross-fence requests by hand. One supervisor mailbox that hit the ten-message limit. Two tracks stalled 8 hours, one on a `--call` probe that ignored `--budget`, one on probes that had already finished.
- **Build time.** Builder loops on the STEP work took 45 minutes to 3 hours (TKT-321); clean-export builds collided on two projects named `Ara3D.Utils` until run sequentially.
- **The server.** The warm checker reached 12.8 GB and wedged once, slowed with every fresh scratch path, and kept a renamed declaration alive at its old path.

## Learned

- The chunk is the unit of work and of merge: at most two hours, one commit, merged alone with a check under ten minutes, the full sweep daily or after every fifth merge. Wave 1 tracks ran 2 hours to 2 days before their first merge; the catalog chunks ran 0.25 to 2.5 hours each with no regression. The rule is now in platonic `CHUNKING.md`, `PLANNING.md` and `TESTING.md`.
- Staging folders beside the library, merged by wiring (a `<Name>Body` and a one-line edit), never by splicing lines two tracks wrote.
- Additions never wait; only a change to an existing contract waits for the wave. Under the earlier freeze, seven tracks added no type and routed everything through `Number` and `Vector3`.
- Status and requests in `TRACK.md`, messages only when blocked.
- Count a contract's callers before changing it (`MeshOutcome` is named on 35 lines but read by 45 callers; `Project` had 25 call sites, so `ProjectDecided` went beside it). Place a type by what it names and a law by what its witnesses name, not by the section it came from.
- Typing a field read by hundreds of literal call sites is cheap when the constructor keeps the string parameter and wraps it: 18 readers changed, not 450.
- The supervisor checks each track's last commit time, not its session status, to catch a stall.
- Servers and pinned CLIs come from clean exports of committed code, built with `-m:1`, and background scripts chain steps with `&&`.
- A merge that changes generated C# needs the regeneration right after it, not once per wave: the corpus run measured a library three merges stale.
- Laws catch arithmetic: the textbook principal-curvature formula lost half its digits at an umbilic; a decided form of `AreaElement > 0` inherited the float's ambiguity at a pole.
