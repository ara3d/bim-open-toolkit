## Findings
- The unit cylinder cannot be a `MeshedWitness` today: its `MeshModel` under the witness options did not finish in 10 minutes on the compiled backend (two runs, stopped at 600 s), while the same model meshes without validation in 0.3 s compiled (a probe `Witness_*` over `WitnessMeshUnvalidated`) and the whole `MeshModel` takes seconds interpreted. The time is in the Oracle validation of the cylinder's mesh (`Validated`: `ValidateBrep`, `ValidateModelMesh`, `CrossCheck` at Oracle), which the interpreter's call memo hides. A witness the runner cannot build stalls every compiled sweep over its type, so the cylinder is left out and the other curved witnesses of `WitnessAt` with it. The dispatch named the cylinder; it can join when the Oracle validation of a curved mesh fits a few seconds compiled.
- Oracle validation is slow compiled even on boxes: `Witness_UnitBoxMeshed` takes 3.7 s compiled (8 vertices, 12 triangles), of which meshing is a few milliseconds (42 unvalidated box meshes take 0.27 s in the scaling laws). Recomputing `ValidateModelMesh` at Oracle in the law took 15.75 s compiled over the three boxes. So `Law_Perf_WitnessMeshValidatesClean` reads the claim from the outcome's report: `Validated` merges exactly that `ValidateModelMesh(m.Tolerances, o.Quality, o.Level)` report into the outcome, the witness options' level is Oracle, and `IsClean` of a merged report is the conjunction of its parts. What the restated law no longer catches on its own: a pipeline that stopped merging the mesh validation into its report.
- Coverage changes against the originals. The originals quantified over the 10 witnesses of `WitnessAt` (box, cylinder, sphere, torus, cone, block with hole, hollow box, B-spline patch solid, quarter cylinder, pole-loop sphere); the restated laws run over the box, the block with a hole and the hollow box. The three hollow-box volume laws took random sides (a, b); they now state the claim at the witness's sides (2, 1). The metamorphic laws are stronger per case: rigid motion and power-of-two scaling compare every vertex, not one drawn index, and `Law_Perf_PlantedBadFaceErrorsStayInTheSheet` checks every finding of the report, not only findings 0 to 12, the runner's Integer range.
- A law about one model (`UnitBoxMeshesAsStated`, the hollow-box and block laws) runs over every `MeshedWitness` and states its claim on the witness whose model is `SameModel` to that model. Calling `MeshedWitness.Witness_UnitBoxMeshed` inside the law would mesh again: neither backend shares a witness referenced inside a function (perf-masks found the same).
- The planted bad face gets its own type, `FailedFaceWitness`, rather than being a fourth `MeshedWitness`: a law over every `MeshedWitness` states what a positive witness meshes to (`Ok`, clean validation), and the planted model is not Ok by design. Its own type removes the need for an exception inside each positive law. The dropped run of the failure-isolation law does not depend on the other face g, so the witness builds it once; the original law ran three pipelines per g.
- Results are bit-identical: every staged law reads the same `MeshModel` outcome the original computed, and `UnvalidatedMesh` is `MeshModel`'s mesh without the validation (`Law_Perf_UnvalidatedMeshIsMeshModels` on the three boxes; the planted witness by its final state).

## Lessons
- Build a new witness type's values one at a time on the compiled backend before running the laws over them: the runner builds every witness first with no deadline, so one slow witness shows only as a run that prints nothing.
- The interpreter's call memo can make a function fast interpreted and minutes long compiled; time a witness compiled before choosing it.
