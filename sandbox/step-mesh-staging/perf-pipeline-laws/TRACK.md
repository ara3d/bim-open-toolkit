## Findings
- **A degenerate element in the bump patch solid's mesh.** `MeshModel` of `Witness_BSplinePatchSolid(0.5)` under the witness options assembles a mesh (bit-identical to `UnvalidatedMesh`, L1's law) on which `ValidateModelMesh` at the Structural level reports one Error: `ElementIsNotDegenerate` at `MeshElement(ElementIndex(1541))`. The original `Law_BumpSolidPassesWitnessLaws` and `Law_BumpSolidMeshesClosedWithinAreaBound` (which needs `Ok`) could never have shown it: their Oracle validation does not finish compiled, and the tsv records both as budget errors. Closed, oriented, well formed, Euler characteristic 2 and the top's area bound all hold.
- **The bump solid cannot be a validated witness.** Compiled, with other builders' runs on the machine: its loop and assembly take 7.7 s (6.7 to 13.9 s across runs); `MeshModel` with Oracle validation did not finish in 9 minutes; at the Structural and Geometric levels it did not finish in 5 minutes, because of `ValidateAssembly` (366 s alone); `ValidateModelMesh` (Structural) took 0.7 s and `ValidateBrep` with `CrossCheck` (Structural) 1.0 s. So its witness is the loop run without validation (the dispatch's fallback), and "meshes Ok" in its laws reads "the loop's findings are clean" (`LoopReport`), the part of the report before validation. Interpreted, building the bump solid and crescent with Oracle validation took 1293 s on the warm server, so the bump solid's laws are checked compiled only.
- **Coverage changes.** The originals ran over every BrepModel witness the runner knows; the restated laws run over the unit box, the block with a hole, the hollow box and the crescent (LoopRunWitness), the bump solid (CostlyLoopRunWitness), L1's three boxes (MeshedWitness) and the planted face (FailedFaceWitness). The curved witnesses of `WitnessAt` (cylinder, sphere, torus, cone, quarter cylinder, pole-loop sphere) are out, as in L1: unvalidated, the cylinder meshes in 0.3 s compiled (L1), so they could join `LoopRunWitness` for the laws that read one run, but each would add about a second to the edge-reversal law, past its budget. The bump solid is left out of the four laws that mesh again (determinism, edge and shell reversal, current outcomes) and of `MeshedAssemblyValidatesStructurally`. The crescent is the only witness whose loop takes a step, so the three step laws are vacuous on the others. Each restated law checks every index of a witness in one case, which is stronger per witness than the runner's Integer sample.
- **Determinism at the Structural level.** `Law_Perf_MeshModelIsDeterministic` keeps its two runs but validates them at the Structural level, since the Oracle validation takes seconds per run compiled (3.2 s for the unit box, L1). That validation reads its inputs and nothing else is `Law_ValidationIsIdempotent`'s claim.
- **`Law_Perf_BumpSolidNeedsNoRound` reads one run.** It does not run the loop under a round limit of 0 (7.7 s); it checks that the run took no step and left nothing pending, so `StateAfter` returns that state under any limit. That rests on `InitialState` reading the options only through `o.Face` (`DiscretizeAll(o.Face.Discretization)`, `FaceOutcomeInRange` calls `MeshFace(f, d, o.Face)`), checked by reading the code, not by a law. The original compared triangle counts; nothing weaker is claimed.
- **Not restated.** `Law_OneRoundResolvesCrossingFace` needs the crescent's `Ok`, which costs its Oracle validation (6.2 s compiled) in the law or a validated witness of a type of its own; it passes interpreted and is not in this chunk's list. `Law_TighterChordalStaysWithinLooserBound` (both) costs its projections, not a second mesh (candidate 36 does not cover it).
- **`EmptyFaceIsDiagnosed` reads the loop's report.** It looks for the Error in `LoopReport`, which holds face meshing's and the pipeline's own findings, the two sources the original's comment names; the outcome's report is that report merged with validation, so the restated law is at least as strong. The planted face's form reads the outcome's report.
- The originals' verdicts are kept or improved; no floating-point result changes (every restated law reads the value the original computed, and the two guard bindings are the same arithmetic).

## Lessons
- Time each witness alone, compiled, before writing laws over it (L1's lesson, which I followed too late): the first design assumed the bump solid could be validated and cost 30 minutes of stalled runs.
- A probe record whose fields are all generatable (a String and a Report) is fed random values, so its verdict says nothing; give it a BrepModel field, and put that field last so the printed case shows the others before truncation.
- A law's time varies two- to five-fold with the other builders' compiled runs on the machine (2.08 s against 0.33 s for the same law); read a number near the budget again on a quieter run before restructuring.
