## Done
- TKT-406, this commit, about 1.3 h against the 2 to 3 h estimate: idea 28 of `docs/plans/step-mesher-ideas-evaluation.md`.
  - Form chosen: neither of the ticket's two. A side counts for the 15 degree side-normal test only when its surface chord is at least twice the minimum length (`EffectiveMinLength`), so that refinement's insertion guard lets it be halved. Why, from the probes below:
    - The cause is layer 04's minimum-length cap, not its angular bound. Layer 04 already splits every circle and ellipse to 15 degrees (`ArcCriteriaCount` takes `EffectiveAngular`), unless `ArcLengthCap` binds: on an arc whose radius is near the minimum length (a tenth of the chordal bound) a quarter circle gets one to five segments. Run C's data agrees: EMMY-W1's circle edges on cylinder+plane have 1 or 4 segments (its `EMMY-W1.base.edges.csv`), which the 15 degree bound alone never gives. The interior cannot be split finer than the same minimum length either, so the flags stayed until the guard refused every insertion.
    - Form (a), raising curve-on-curved-surface counts to ceil(arc / 15 degrees), breaks that cap: the cap exists so no chord is shorter than the minimum length, and the edges it binds on are exactly the ones form (a) would split.
    - Form (b), refusing a triangle whose boundary segment turns by over twice the angle, does not help: on the quarter cylinder of radius 1 at chordal 6 (two 45 degree chords) it gave 22 triangles against 18, and the triangles across the other two sides stayed flagged; at chordal 3 the chords turn 18 degrees, under twice the angle, so it changed nothing, while 59 of 92 triangles stayed flagged.
    - A face-wide raise (the angle to the largest boundary-segment turn) was also measured: better at chordal 6 and 4, worse at chordal 3 (92 to 100 triangles, 53 still flagged), and it changed the unit cylinder at chordal 1/100 (304 to 280 triangles) only because its 15 degree chords turn by 15 degrees plus a rounding unit. Rejected.
    - The chosen rule is layer 04's rule carried to the face (an arc's chord is split only while both halves stay at least the minimum length). It changes only the per-face test, so every edge discretization, and with it closure and orientation across faces, is untouched. It changes no mesh of any library witness probed (the 42 triangulated faces at chordal 1/100, the sphere and both tori at 0.05, the cone at 0.01): no side that turns by more than 15 degrees is that short there.
    - The strip (TKT-405) does not need it: its faces do not refine. The 9-to-16-edge cylinders and every other curved face that refines do.

## Lessons
- A torus or cone witness in the interpreter takes many minutes, and the warm server keeps running a request after its client gives up, so every later interpreter request queues behind it. Probe curved pole witnesses on the compiled backend from the start.
- The runner prints only the first failing case of a law, so a probe needs one law per witness (`p.Name != "X"`) and a record whose first fields are the numbers wanted.
- Measuring the ticket's two forms before building either decided the chunk: both were plausible on paper, and the probes showed one breaks layer 04's cap and the other changes nothing at 18 degrees.
